## 2. Option-Dealer Hedging Paper

# Shared Funding Constraints and Variational Equilibrium in Option Dealer Hedging

**Authors:** Yuvraj Malik and Nicholas Bo You Chin  
**First Author:** Yuvraj Malik  
**Status:** Preprint; under review at *Mathematics and Financial Economics*  
**Year:** 2026

## Overview

This repository contains the preprint and supporting materials for:

> **Shared Funding Constraints and Variational Equilibrium in Option Dealer Hedging**

The paper develops a theoretical model of option dealers who hedge their inventories while competing for a shared and limited funding capacity.

Unlike models in which each dealer solves an independent optimization problem, the feasible hedging decisions in this setting are coupled through a common balance-sheet or funding constraint. This coupling leads naturally to a generalized Nash equilibrium, represented mathematically as a variational equilibrium.

The framework is used to study how shared funding scarcity affects dealer hedging, equilibrium prices, and deviations from frictionless option values.

## Economic Motivation

Option dealers dynamically hedge exposures generated by customer order flow and inventory accumulation. During normal market conditions, funding and balance-sheet constraints may have limited effects on hedging decisions.

During stressed market conditions, however:

- dealers may compete for limited financing capacity;
- hedging costs may increase;
- balance-sheet constraints may become binding;
- dealer demand for the underlying asset may become strongly coupled;
- option prices may deviate from frictionless benchmarks.

The model studies these effects within a unified equilibrium framework.

## Model

Each dealer chooses a hedging strategy to minimize an objective containing components such as:

- inventory risk;
- hedging costs;
- market impact;
- deviations from a target hedge;
- funding or balance-sheet usage.

Dealer decisions are coupled through a shared constraint of the form

\[
\sum_{i=1}^{N} g_i(x_i) \leq B,
\]

where:

- \(x_i\) is dealer \(i\)'s hedging decision;
- \(g_i(x_i)\) measures the funding or balance-sheet usage generated by that decision;
- \(B\) is the aggregate funding capacity available to the dealer system.

The equilibrium is formulated as a variational inequality over the jointly feasible strategy set.

## Main Contributions

The paper makes the following contributions:

1. **Variational-equilibrium formulation**

   Multi-dealer option hedging under a shared funding constraint is formulated as a generalized Nash equilibrium and represented through a variational inequality.

2. **Existence and uniqueness**

   Conditions based on convexity, continuity, coercivity, and monotonicity are provided under which an equilibrium exists and is unique.

3. **Funding shadow price**

   The Lagrange multiplier associated with the shared balance-sheet constraint is interpreted as the endogenous shadow price of scarce dealer funding.

4. **Option-pricing distortions**

   The model derives bounds on the difference between dealer-implied option values and frictionless benchmark prices.

5. **Comparative statics**

   The analysis shows how tighter funding capacity can increase hedging costs, alter equilibrium hedge positions, and widen pricing distortions.

6. **System-wide coupling**

   The framework demonstrates how the inventory and funding position of one dealer can affect the equilibrium behavior of all other dealers through the common constraint.
